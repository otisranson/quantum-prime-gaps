## Current verdict (2026-08-11)

**The mechanism does not beat trivial baselines on backward verification, at any
`--top-k` setting tried.** Fourier extrapolation assumes the known window has
meaningful periodic structure; the first-40-primes gap sequence doesn't appear to have
enough of it for this to predict better than just guessing "the average gap" or
"whatever the last gap was." This is a negative result, not a bug — see
[What works / what doesn't](#what-works--what-doesnt) below.

## Backward verification: `--top-k` sweep

Predicting gaps 40–49 (→ primes 41–50) from only the first 40 primes. Baselines for
this split: **mean-gap MAE = 3.277**, **repeat-last-gap MAE = 3.600**.

| `--top-k` | Model MAE | Beats both baselines? |
|---:|---:|:---:|
| `None` (all frequencies) | 3.900 | ❌ No |
| 1 | 3.277 | ❌ No (ties mean-gap exactly — see note) |
| 2 | 3.726 | ❌ No |
| 3 | 3.888 | ❌ No |
| 4 | 4.200 | ❌ No |
| 5 | 4.713 | ❌ No |
| 8 | 4.345 | ❌ No |
| 12 | 3.910 | ❌ No |
| 19 (max, half the 39-gap window) | 3.938 | ❌ No |

**Note on `--top-k 1`:** its MAE (3.277) exactly equals the mean-gap baseline. That's
expected, not a coincidence worth chasing: the single strongest frequency component in
this data is the DC bin (index 0), and reconstructing from DC alone is mathematically
identical to predicting the mean of the known gaps at every future step. This is a
useful sanity check that the reconstruction math degrades to the right limiting case,
not evidence for or against the mechanism.

No `--top-k` value tested crosses either baseline. There isn't an obvious trend
(MAE isn't monotonic in `--top-k`), which is itself informative — it doesn't look like
"just needs a bit more/less truncation" would fix it.

## What works / what doesn't

**Works:**
- The plumbing is solid: amplitude encoding, gate-level QFT/inverse-QFT, and the
  classical spectral-extrapolation formula all pass their round-trip hard checks. The
  backward-verification harness itself (same code path forward and backward, real
  baselines, no cherry-picking) is doing exactly what it's supposed to — producing a
  trustworthy negative result instead of a misleading positive one.

**Doesn't work (yet):**
- Fourier extrapolation of the raw gap sequence has no demonstrated predictive edge
  over "guess the mean" or "repeat the last value" on the one holdout split tested
  (first 40 → next 10). This held across every `--top-k` tried, not just the default.
- 39–49 samples is a short window for spectral methods in general; the "one period"
  assumption baked into the `--top-k None` default is doing a lot of unearned work.

**Not yet tried:**
- Detrending (primes grow, so gaps trend up on average — subtracting a linear/log
  trend before FFT might matter) or working in log-gap space.
- A larger backward-verification sample: only one 40/10 split has been tested; sliding
  the split point (e.g. predict gaps 31–40 from the first 30, etc.) would show whether
  3.9-ish MAE is typical or this split was unusually hard.
- Non-Fourier baselines with actual number-theoretic content (e.g. weighting by the
  Cramér heuristic / prime-counting density near the candidate) instead of a flat
  sieve-nearest-neighbor snap.
